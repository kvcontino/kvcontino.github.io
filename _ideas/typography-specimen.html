Typography Specimen

One typeface, two reds, three devices. Everything the stylesheet can do, on one page, so a regression has somewhere to show itself.

Sorts Mill Goudy (Barry Schwartz, 2010), a revival of Frederic Goudy's Goudy Oldstyle of 1915 — the face used throughout the Blade Runner main titles.

The crawl

The source text, set the way the film sets it: sentence case, first-line indents, no space between paragraphs, generous word spacing, ragged right. Proper names take small capitals on introduction. A term of art is italicised every time it appears and red only the first time.

Early in the 21st Century, the Tyrell Corporation advanced Robot evolution into the Nexus phase — a being virtually identical to a human — known as a Replicant.

The Nexus 6 Replicants were superior in strength and agility, and at least equal in intelligence, to the genetic engineers who created them. Note that the second paragraph indents and the first does not, that Nexus is no longer being introduced but is set in small capitals again here only to show the glyphs, and that Replicants has lost its red.

Title cards

An inline run of capitals takes the tracking and a hair less size. Small capitals — Fort Drum, OneCare Vermont, Arcadia — take the same tracking. Lowercase never does.

A third-rank heading is small capitals

Which distinguishes it from the second rank by case as well as size, so the page never shows three sizes of the same shouting. Body copy resumes here at full contrast, with a link carrying a red rule beneath it rather than red letters, so the signal survives colour blindness and keeps the text at 17:1.

In running prose the figures are oldstyle and proportional, as Goudy intended: 1915, 1982, 21st Century, 0123456789. In the table above they are lining and — because every lining figure in this font is 527 units wide — already tabular.

An aside takes the display red as a rule down its left edge. The rule is a graphic, not text, so the low-contrast red is fine there.

Ordinary italic emphasis stays the same colour as the body. The face has no bold weight, so strong is italic too — which means emphasis in this design is a single gesture and cannot be stacked.
